# Chapter One: The Quiet Machine

It was a small device, no larger than a deck of cards, and it held **every book** she had ever wanted to read. The screen showed a single word at a time.

At first it felt *strange*, like listening to someone who never paused for breath. Then, after a few minutes, the words stopped being words and became a stream of meaning.

"Faster," she whispered, and pressed the button. The machine obliged.

# Chapter Two: Speed

Most people read around two hundred and fifty words per minute. With practice, *rapid serial visual presentation* lets the eye stay perfectly still while the text comes to it. The red letter marks the **optimal recognition point**, the place the eye naturally wants to land.

She learned to hold the left button to scroll back through a sentence she had missed, and to let go to carry on reading. Chapters announced themselves slowly, giving her time to breathe between them.

Some sentences (like this one, with an aside) carry a thought inside brackets.

By evening she had finished the whole book.
